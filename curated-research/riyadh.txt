Rental cars:
Key Car Rental (key.sa) - budget tier. Saudi local operator with branches across the Kingdom and online booking. Locally established rather than international; no independent review score found, treat reputation as unconfirmed.
Budget Rent A Car - budget/mid tier. International chain, present in Riyadh.
Enterprise - mid tier. Both airport and neighbourhood branches in Riyadh. Part of the Enterprise/National/Alamo group.
Alamo - mid tier. Desk at King Khalid Intl Terminal 5 (RUH), cars, SUVs and vans.
National - mid tier, same group as Enterprise/Alamo.
Hertz - mid/premium tier. Downtown Olaya branch plus airport pickup.
Sixt - premium tier. Fleet spans luxury cars, SUVs and minivans. Minimum rental age 21, higher for some categories with an underage driver fee. Branch at The District, King Fahad Road, Al Olaya.
Lumi - Saudi company headquartered in Riyadh, rents cars, buses and motorcycles. Useful for larger groups.
On reputation: car-rental comparison sites list aggregate customer scores for the international chains in Riyadh (National, Enterprise and Sixt all scoring well). These are booking-aggregator scores, not Trustpilot or an official rating, so treat them as a soft signal only, not verification. No Saudi government licensing registry listing was located for any of these during this research, so do not describe any of them as "licensed" or "government approved" to a customer.
Booking.com data indicates Enterprise, Sixt and Alamo are the most common brands offering one-way rentals in Riyadh.
Practical: there is no public transit link from King Khalid airport to downtown, so a rental car or a booked transfer is the realistic option.

Attractions:
Diriyah / At-Turaif - open. Saturday to Thursday 10:00 to 24:00, Friday 14:00 to 24:00, last entry 23:30. Entry is via a Diriyah Pass covering At-Turaif and Bujairi Terrace, SAR 50 to 100 per person depending on time and season, free for children 12 and under. The pass fee is redeemable as dining credit at any Bujairi Terrace restaurant, so for a group that plans to eat there it is effectively free entry, worth telling the customer. Paid parking at Bujairi and Samhan, about SAR 30 for three hours. Group visits need at least one week's notice, Saturday to Thursday 10:00 to 14:00 only, relevant if the party is large.
National Museum & Al Murabba - open. Monday to Saturday 09:00 to 19:00, Thursday 09:00 to 22:00, Friday 14:00 to 22:00, CLOSED SUNDAY. General admission is free, but tickets are reserved through the museum's official booking system, so book ahead rather than turning up. The Sunday closure is the planning trap here.
Six Flags Qiddiya City - open, opened 31 December 2025. Adult tickets from SAR 325, children from SAR 275, under-4s free, and a family bundle around SAR 1,200 for five people. Hours are evening-weighted, typically 16:00 to midnight and later on some nights, which suits Riyadh daytime heat well.
AquaRabia (Qiddiya City) - open, opened 23 April 2026, the largest water park in the Middle East. Tickets from SAR 325. Operates roughly 12:00 to 20:00 with last entry around 19:00, though one source reported Wednesday to Sunday operation rather than daily, so confirm the specific weekday before committing a family to it. Separately, there are reports that Fridays are reserved for women and children under 8 only - this was NOT confirmed in this research and would materially change a family booking, so the team must verify it directly with the park before scheduling a Friday visit. Fully outdoor, so heat timing matters.
Boulevard World - SEASONAL AND CURRENTLY CLOSED. It ran 11 October 2025 to 2 May 2026 as part of Riyadh Season and is shut outside that window. Riyadh Season typically restarts around October, so a trip before then cannot include it and a trip after depends on the new season being announced. During season it runs daily 16:00 to 01:00, entry from about SAR 30, under-5s free, with country pavilions, rides and a very large number of restaurants. Do not put this in an itinerary without checking the current season dates first, this is the single most likely stale item in this city.
Kingdom Centre & KAFD - not checked in this pass, ran the budget on the seasonal and newly-opened sites first. Kingdom Centre's Sky Bridge is a long-standing fixture; confirm current Sky Bridge hours and ticket price at booking time.

Restaurants:
Budget tier - Al Hayat (Pakistani, budget-friendly), Raseef 4 (manakish and pies, quick and cheap), B Mart (street-style hot pot, around SAR 20). Riyadh has many small local places serving mandi, kabsa and harees on shared platters, which suits family groups and is genuinely inexpensive.
Mid tier - Najd Village (traditional Saudi, majlis-style seating, long-established, a good "authentic Saudi meal" pick for visitors), Piatto (Italian, family-friendly, daily offers and lunch specials).
Upscale tier - La Petite Maison, Zuma, Koi, Spago by Wolfgang Puck, Myazu (Japanese), TAKYA (Saudi fine dining), plus several international names including Roka, COYA and Nobu-tier steakhouses in Olaya. Booking ahead is essential at effectively all of these.
Note: our own curated dining list already covers Riyadh, so treat these as supplementary options for filling a price tier the curated list misses rather than as replacements.

Flights:
RUH (King Khalid International) is a hub for Saudia, Riyadh Air (the newer national carrier), and low-cost carriers flynas and flyadeal. Around 61 airlines serve the airport with direct service to roughly 126 destinations, so most international travellers can reach Riyadh directly rather than connecting through Jeddah. The busiest routes are Jeddah and Cairo. No flight times or prices researched, the team prices these separately.
