Rental cars:
Key Car Rental - budget tier. Saudi local operator, present at Jeddah airport. Caution on its rating: one comparison site shows it at 10.0/10, but that score is based on only 2 reviews and is statistically meaningless, do not repeat it to a customer as evidence of quality.
Budget - budget/mid tier. Desk at King Abdulaziz International (JED).
HANCO - Saudi local operator, mid tier, long-established in the Kingdom.
Surprice - budget/mid tier, present at JED.
Europcar - mid tier, international chain.
Avis - mid tier. The highest-volume review base found at JED, averaging about 7.5 across roughly 7,100 customers, which is a far more trustworthy signal than the small-sample scores above.
Enterprise - mid tier, around 8.7 across roughly 2,300 customers, the best combination of score and sample size found. IMPORTANT LOGISTICS: Enterprise sits in Terminal 1 while most other desks are at the South Terminal, so a customer sent to the wrong terminal will lose an hour, confirm the terminal on the booking.
Sixt - premium tier, about 4.5 stars from roughly 273 reviews at Jeddah Airport.
Lumi - Saudi operator, cars plus larger vehicles, useful for bigger family groups.
Indicative pricing seen across aggregators: roughly USD 25-32/day booked well ahead, around USD 42 on a weekend and up to about USD 69 midweek. Treat as a planning ballpark only, not a quotable price.
On licensing: no Saudi government licensed-operator registry listing was located for any of these during this research. Do not describe any of them as "licensed" or "approved" to a customer; the international chains carry brand-level accountability, which is a reasonable thing to say, but it is not the same as a verified local licence.

Attractions:
Al-Balad (Jeddah Historic District) - open, UNESCO World Heritage listed since 2014. Open daily roughly 07:00 to 03:00, but the practical advice is to go after 17:00: before then many shops are shut and the heat is punishing. No general admission ticket found for simply walking the district; guided tours are widely sold at roughly USD 109-150 per adult if the customer wants one, which is worth flagging as an optional add-on rather than a requirement. Three restored heritage hotels now operate inside the district (Beit Jokhdar, Beit Al-Rayes, Beit Kedwan), restored to UNESCO guidelines, which can be an appealing and distinctive stay for the right customer.
Jeddah Corniche & King Fahd Fountain - open and free. The fountain runs roughly 18:00 to midnight daily, and is at its best after dark when it is lit. Two genuine caveats: it pauses for an annual maintenance window, and it does not run when southerly winds make operation unsafe, so it can never be promised as guaranteed on a specific evening. The Corniche itself is freely accessible with no hours or tickets, an easy no-cost evening for a family.
Red Sea diving & boat days - operating. Dive operations run mainly out of Obhur, a creek north of the city, and most operators will collect guests from their hotel, which simplifies logistics. Coral and water clarity are reported as very good. Specific season windows and per-trip pricing were not confirmed in this research, so the team should price and confirm dates with an operator directly rather than quoting anything.
Jeddah Waterfront - freely accessible public waterfront, no tickets or set hours. Best in the evening for the same heat reason as everything else here.

Flights and ground connections:
JED (King Abdulaziz International) is the main Red Sea gateway and the primary arrival point for Umrah travellers. Served by Saudia, flynas, flyadeal and a wide range of international carriers.
The Haramain High-Speed Railway has a station INSIDE Terminal 1 at JED, which is the single most useful logistics fact for this city. It connects Jeddah Airport to Makkah in roughly 1 hour and to Madinah in roughly 2 hours at speeds up to 300 km/h, and also serves Jeddah city (Sulaymaniyah station) and King Abdullah Economic City. For any Umrah or multi-city itinerary this is usually better than road transfer, and it means a customer arriving at JED does not necessarily need a car at all. Confirm current schedules and fares at booking time.
No flight times or prices researched, the team prices these separately.

Typical costs (researched ranges, use as honest guides, never as quotes):
Eating out, general Jeddah price bands by category, not per-restaurant quotes: street food roughly SAR 10-25 a meal; fast-food combo around SAR 30; local traditional places roughly SAR 25-40; mid-range sit-down restaurants roughly SAR 40-90 per person, with mains around SAR 35-75; international and upscale dining roughly SAR 50-150 per person. Mixing street food and mid-range places, a realistic daily food budget is about SAR 120-200 per person.
IMPORTANT on our four curated Jeddah restaurants (San Carlo, Sura, Sultan's Steakhouse, Lusin): no per-restaurant pricing was found for any of them. They sit in the international/upscale category, so the SAR 50-150 per person band is the honest guide to quote, explicitly as a category range rather than as those restaurants' actual prices. Do not state a specific per-head figure for any of the four.
Diving and boat days out of Obhur: a single dive generally runs about SAR 300-500, with the wider range across operators and sites roughly SAR 300-700. A beginner discovery dive is about SAR 300-400; a certified diver's trip about SAR 350-500 per dive. One operator lists a 4-hour scuba experience at SAR 600. Multi-day packages including accommodation run roughly SAR 1,500-3,000. Operators are concentrated around Sharm Obhur and most collect from hotels. These are advertised ranges gathered across operators, so quote them as a guide and tell the customer to confirm the exact figure with the operator when booking.
Guided Al-Balad walking tours: sold widely at roughly USD 109-150 per adult. Walking the district independently is free, there is no admission charge, so this is genuinely optional rather than required.
Hotels per night: reported averages vary widely by platform, so treat these as broad guidance only. 3-star roughly USD 57-101; 4-star roughly USD 92-243; 5-star roughly USD 436-863. In riyals, accommodation spans roughly SAR 550 for a solid 3-star up to about SAR 3,800 for a luxury property. Add 5% municipality fee and 15% VAT to quoted rates.
